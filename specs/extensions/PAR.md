# The PAR method
Problem, Action, Result

P.A.R. is a close cousin of the STAR method, used to structure resume bullet points and behavioral interview answers around three components instead of four. Breaking down the formula:

Problem, describe the specific challenge, obstacle, or situation you faced. This sets the context, similar to STAR's "Situation," but framing it explicitly as a "Problem" puts the emphasis on a challenge that needed solving rather than a neutral scene-setter — useful when you want to foreground the difficulty you overcame.

Action, explain the specific steps you personally took to address the problem, including your reasoning and decision-making. This absorbs STAR's separate "Task" step into the setup, so there's no distinct goal-statement beat before the action begins.

Result, detail the positive, ideally quantifiable outcome your actions produced, and what it demonstrated or taught you.

PAR is often chosen over STAR for its brevity and directness: dropping the "Task" step removes redundancy when the task is already obvious from the problem, making PAR well suited to tight resume bullets and interview answers where you want to get to the action and payoff quickly. STAR remains more common in formal, structured behavioral interviews where interviewers explicitly want the task/goal called out separately from the situation.
